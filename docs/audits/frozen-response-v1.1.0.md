# v1.1.0 frozen tangent variance

**Current.** The W01 checkpoint implements the stationary, unconditional increment variance of the locally frozen imbalance root in operational time. Its scientific reference is [Angstmann and Gebbie, arXiv:2607.05011v4, §§4–5 and Appendices A and D](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.05011v4).

## Two independent calculations

Let $V_\Delta=\operatorname{Var}(Y(u+\Delta)-Y(u))$ and $V_Y=\operatorname{Var}(Y(u))$. The coefficient in Eq. (47) is $V_\Delta/\Delta$. All calculations use the causal kernel and forcing covariance of Eqs. (46) and (48), a stationary start, and constant frozen parameters.

* **Frequency domain.** For $s=\nu+i\omega$, the kernel transform is $e^{-\nu\tau_0}\operatorname{erfcx}(\sqrt{s\tau_0})/(2\sqrt{Ds})$. The forcing spectrum follows from the Fourier transform of $A_m(|h|+\tau_0)^{-\gamma}$. For $|\omega|\tau_0\ge1$ the implementation uses its positive mixture of exponential covariances with a generalized Gauss–Laguerre rule. The increment filter uses $2\sin^2(x/2)$ for $1-\cos x$ near zero and oscillatory quadrature for the high-frequency cosine tail.
* **Time domain.** Define $W(h)=\int_0^\infty g(r)g(r+h)\,dr$ for $h\ge0$. The direct calculation integrates $2W(h)[2C(h)-C(|h-\Delta|)-C(h+\Delta)]/\mathcal L^2$ over positive $h$. Integrating $2W(h)C(h)/\mathcal L^2$ separately gives $V_Y$. Both integrals use the original covariance in time. The integration splits at the lag cusp and resolves the cutoff on either side. The exponential kernel tail is truncated at a minimum of 45 resilience times.
* **Zero-cutoff comparison.** The separate evaluator integrates Eq. (21) over the full crossover. Its finite-lag prediction is $A_m\Delta^{1-\gamma}\mathcal F_\gamma(\nu\Delta)/(D\mathcal L^2)$. The figure also shows $\chi_\gamma=\gamma-d\log\mathcal F_\gamma/d\log z$, the local negative log-slope of the zero-cutoff variance rate.

## Verified evidence

The [48-case comparison](../../outputs/v2/frozen-variance-checks.csv) crosses $\gamma\in\{0.3,0.55,0.8\}$, $\nu\in\{0.25,2\}$, $\tau_0\in\{0.001,0.08\}$ and four lags per configuration: $0.01\tau_0$, $0.02/\nu$, $1/\nu$ and $50/\nu$. The largest relative discrepancy between the two methods was $3.84\times10^{-11}$. Tightening spectral tolerance from $10^{-8}$ to $10^{-10}$ and doubling rate nodes from 64 to 128 changed the result by at most $3.75\times10^{-11}$. Tightening independent time-domain tolerance from $10^{-7}$ to $10^{-10}$ changed the variance by at most $8.72\times10^{-12}$. These are observed discrepancies on the grid.

For $\gamma=1/2$, $\nu=A_m=D=\mathcal L=1$, endpoint log-slopes of $V_\Delta$ illustrate the three scales.

| Operational window | Cutoff | Measured slope | Target |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| $10^{-5}\le\Delta\le10^{-4}$ | $0.1$ | 1.999812 | 2 |
| $10^{-4}\le\Delta\le10^{-2}$ | $10^{-12}$ | 0.500374 | $1-\gamma$ |
| $10^4\le\Delta\le10^6$ | $0.1$ | 0.001321 | 0 |

At $z=10^6$, the finite-cutoff variance reaches 0.999326 of $2V_Y$ for $\tau_0=0.1$. At the same lag it reaches only 0.214916 of the zero-cutoff high-$z$ prediction. For $\tau_0=10^{-12}$ the latter ratio is 0.999638. The slope and the plateau normalization therefore test different aspects of the approximation.

For the stationary Gaussian tangent system, Eq. (68) gives a conditional-to-unconditional second-moment ratio of $1+[V_\Delta/(4V_Y)](y^2/V_Y-1)$. At $\tau_0=0.1$ and $z=0.001$, the ratios at $y=0$ and $y=2\sqrt{V_Y}$ are 0.999999 and 1.000003. At $z=10^6$ they are 0.500337 and 2.498989. The unconditional high-$z$ plateau alone cannot identify a state-conditioned pricing coefficient.

[Figure SV2-F01](../../figures/v2/response-crossover.svg) and its [numerical data](../../outputs/v2/response-crossover.csv) use 121 log-spaced lags, $\gamma=1/2$, $\nu=A_m=D=\mathcal L=1$ and the indicated cutoffs. The central log-difference for $\chi_\gamma$ uses 241 lags and second-order one-sided endpoints. Comparing with 121 lags changes the slope by at most 0.000873. This is a deterministic M0 calculation with no random seed. The figure and both CSVs are generated by the same script.

```sh

python -W error -m unittest discover -s tests -v

```

**Open limitations.** Finite-grid agreement gives numerical evidence for the frozen stationary model. The mesoscopic low-$z$ window requires $\tau_0\ll\Delta\ll\nu^{-1}$. The high-$z$ zero-cutoff prefactor additionally requires $\nu\tau_0\ll1$. The Gaussian conditioning calculation applies to bounded standardized tangent states. Collective translation, actual-root diffusion, calendar-time clocks and pricing-measure selection lie beyond this checkpoint.
